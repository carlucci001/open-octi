# Press release playbook

## Required shape

1. Headline: active voice, specific news, 90 characters or fewer.
2. Optional deck: one sentence adding context rather than repeating the headline.
3. Dateline: `CITY, State — Month Day, Year —`.
4. Lede: 35 words or fewer answering who did what, when, where, and why readers should care.
5. Body: inverted-pyramid order. Put the most newsworthy verified facts first; background and lower-priority detail later.
6. Quote: one real quote from a named person with a title. Do not fabricate or polish beyond the speaker's stated meaning.
7. Proof point: one sourced number, date, credential, result, or concrete example. If its source is missing, ask.
8. Boilerplate: a short factual description of the organization.
9. Media contact: name, role, email, and phone only when supplied.
10. End mark: `###`.

Target 300–500 words. Follow current AP style for capitalization, dates, numerals, titles, punctuation, and attribution. Use short paragraphs and plain language.

## Pickup value

Editors are more likely to consider a release with a clear news hook, direct reader impact, a credible local angle, useful data, timely availability, or a genuine exclusive. Hype, unsupported superlatives, vague announcements, buried facts, long blocks of text, and generic mass pitches weaken it.

## Pitch email

- Subject: 60 characters or fewer; lead with the news, not the sender.
- Sentence 1: the verified news and timing.
- Sentence 2: why it fits this reporter, referencing actual recent coverage.
- Sentence 3: a link to the release and a concrete offer for interview, data, images, or local detail.
- Never claim familiarity with work that is not in the contact record.
