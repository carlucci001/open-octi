# Annotated release pattern bank

These are structural examples, not reusable claims. Replace every bracketed fact with client-confirmed information.

## 1. Product launch

**Headline:** `[Company] Launches [Product] to Help [Audience] [Concrete Outcome]`

**Pattern:** Dateline and lede state availability, audience, problem, and date. Body explains two concrete capabilities, includes a customer-safe proof point, a founder quote, availability, boilerplate, and contact.

**Why it works:** The new product and reader outcome are immediate; jargon and “revolutionary” claims are absent.

## 2. Executive hire

**Headline:** `[Company] Names [Person] as [Title]`

**Pattern:** Lede states the appointment date and mandate. Add verified prior experience, one quote from the appointee or CEO, and the business reason for the role.

**Why it works:** The personnel change is connected to a specific company priority rather than treated as a résumé dump.

## 3. Funding

**Headline:** `[Company] Raises [$Amount] to Expand [Specific Capability]`

**Pattern:** Identify round type, close date, named investors, use of proceeds, and one sourced operating measure. Quote a named executive or investor only from supplied language.

**Why it works:** Amount, backers, and use of funds answer the editor's first questions.

## 4. Award

**Headline:** `[Organization] Recognizes [Company] for [Specific Work]`

**Pattern:** Name the awarding body, award, date, selection basis, and source URL. Explain the work recognized and include one grounded quote.

**Why it works:** Independent validation is identifiable; “award-winning” is never left unsupported.

## 5. Event

**Headline:** `[Organization] to Host [Event] in [Place] on [Date]`

**Pattern:** Lede includes who, what, date, place, audience, and purpose. Body supplies schedule, speakers, access or registration facts, community relevance, quote, boilerplate, and contact.

**Why it works:** A calendar editor can use the essential logistics immediately.

## 6. Opening

**Headline:** `[Business] Opens [Location or Facility] in [Community]`

**Pattern:** State opening date, exact public location, services, hours, jobs or investment only when sourced, and what changes for local customers. Quote the owner or local partner.

**Why it works:** It leads with a concrete local change and usable public details.

## 7. Partnership

**Headline:** `[Company A] and [Company B] Partner to [Specific Outcome]`

**Pattern:** Explain each party's role, start date, who benefits, measurable scope, and what is new compared with prior practice. Include one approved quote from each party when available.

**Why it works:** The arrangement produces an observable result instead of merely announcing alignment.

## 8. Milestone

**Headline:** `[Company] Reaches [Verified Milestone] in [Time Period]`

**Pattern:** Name the milestone and source, give comparison context, explain its relevance to customers or the community, and avoid implying causation the data cannot support.

**Why it works:** The number is sourced, contextualized, and tied to reader impact.

## 9. Community initiative

**Headline:** `[Company] Begins [Program] for [Community or Group]`

**Pattern:** State who is eligible, what they receive, dates, capacity, partner organizations, and how to participate. Include a quote from a named program lead.

**Why it works:** Service information comes before corporate credit-taking.

## 10. Study or data

**Headline:** `[Study] Finds [Specific Result] Among [Population]`

**Pattern:** Lead with the finding, methodology, sample size, field dates, limitations, and source link. Distinguish correlation from causation and include a qualified spokesperson.

**Why it works:** Reporters can evaluate the evidence without requesting basic methodology.

## 11. Expansion

**Headline:** `[Company] Expands [Service or Operations] to [Market]`

**Pattern:** Identify the new geography or capacity, effective date, customer impact, verified staffing or investment, operational reason, and local contact.

**Why it works:** “Expansion” is defined with dates, scope, and tangible availability.

## 12. Rebrand

**Headline:** `[Former Name] Becomes [New Name] as [Business Change] Takes Effect`

**Pattern:** State effective date, what changes, what stays the same, the strategic reason, customer action required, approved quote, boilerplate, and contact.

**Why it works:** Customers and editors get practical consequences, not a design-language essay.
